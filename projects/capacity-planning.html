Section 1: Technical Architecture & Systems Engineering

This section describes the platform as a controlled planning system: normalized operational data, explicit scheduling state, protected exceptions, optimization services, and a deployment path designed for reliability.

Enterprise data architecture

The foundation is a 38-table normalized schema in MySQL 8.4. Core entities separate people, roles, branches, branch rosters, skills, contracts, calendars, shifts, leave, assignments, optimization runs, recommendations, and outcomes. Foreign keys and unique constraints prevent duplicate memberships, invalid branch relationships, overlapping effective records, and orphaned scheduling rows.

Roles define capability and permission boundaries. Branch rosters express who is available in a location and time window, while historical snapshotting preserves the exact roster, target, and rule inputs used for a planning decision. This keeps current truth separate from historical evidence and makes audits reproducible instead of dependent on a spreadsheet that may have changed later.

Dual-layer scheduling state machine

Shift is the reusable master template: role, branch, recurrence, target pattern, and standard duration. StagingShift is the live calendar row: a dated operational instance with assignment, status, leave interaction, and synchronization metadata. Decoupling the two prevents a rolling update from mutating a live commitment while a planner or manager is editing it.

SHIFT TEMPLATE → SIX-MONTH REPLICATION → STAGINGSHIFT CALENDAR

The six-month automated replication engine expands templates into dated rows, applies calendar rules, checks existing protected state, and writes only the fields that the synchronization owns. Idempotent keys and bounded updates make reruns safe, reduce race conditions, and prevent duplicate rows or silent data corruption.

Protection logic and conflict resolution

The sync state machine treats approved vacations, half-day leaves, national bank holidays, paid time off, and temporary reassignments as protected operational statuses. A rolling sync can refresh a template-derived value only when the live row remains eligible. When rules conflict, the precedence order preserves explicit human and legal commitments first, then temporary operational overrides, then generated defaults.

Conflicts are surfaced as explainable outcomes rather than overwritten. The system records the source status, attempted change, effective date, and resolution, allowing a planner to understand why a generated shift was skipped, shortened, reassigned, or left untouched.

Mathematical optimization engine, M3

M3 models mobile workforce routing as a dynamic programming problem over reachable assignments and time windows. Travel allowance matrices encode movement cost, reach-map graphs encode feasible transitions, and country-scoped batch runs keep calendars, labor rules, and geography bounded to the correct operating context.

A swap-refinement heuristic then tests local exchanges after the primary solution. It accepts a swap only when feasibility is preserved and the objective improves, balancing coverage, travel, role fit, and operational penalties. This produces a practical solution quickly while retaining deterministic constraints that planners can explain.

Vacation-cover recommender

The recommender minimizes expected loss rather than simply choosing the nearest available person. A candidate score combines skill compatibility, travel cost, branch impact, contracted availability, continuity, and the penalty for leaving another slot uncovered. Hard constraints remove infeasible candidates before ranking begins.

Each recommendation passes through a DecisionOutcome grading pipeline. The platform compares the proposed cover with actual execution data, including the eventual assignee, realized travel, accepted coverage, and operational deviation. A recommendation is considered within tolerance when its cost is within ±25€ of the observed decision, creating a measurable calibration loop instead of a black-box success claim.

Infrastructure and modern web stack

  • TanStack Query 5 manages server state, cache invalidation, loading states, and mutation feedback in the planner UI.

Section 2: Technical Product Management & Operational Strategy

This section connects the architecture to the operating model: who experiences the problem, how targets are calibrated, which decisions improve, and how adoption is managed.

The enterprise problem space

Decentralized Excel models across dozens of European branch networks created incompatible assumptions and no dependable system of record. Scheduling blind spots hid coverage gaps, payroll discrepancies emerged from inconsistent hours, compliance breaches became difficult to prove or prevent, and planners lost hours or days to manual reconciliation and replanning.

The product response was not spreadsheet replacement for its own sake. It was a governed operational model that made local decisions fast while giving regional and executive users a shared view of capacity, risk, and evidence.

Multi-stakeholder persona and workflow matrix

  • Branch Managers: adjust daily rosters, sign off leave, resolve local exceptions, and see exactly which shifts remain uncovered.
  • Regional Operations Directors: monitor cross-branch mobility, compare reach and demand, and receive coverage-gap alerts before they become service failures.
  • Executive Workforce Planners: forecast macro capacity, align labor budgets, compare country-level scenarios, and inspect the assumptions behind targets.

The journey is layered: local users need low-friction action, regional users need exception visibility, and executives need aggregation without losing traceability back to a branch-level decision.

Target calibration and unit economics

Pricing and capacity targets translate operational branch capacity into discrete role targets. The model combines working-day calendars, branch demand, role mix, contract assumptions, and evaluation slots of 20 or 30 minutes. Headcount is kept distinct from skill composition, so two teams with equal size are not treated as equivalent when their capabilities differ.

Target capacity
working days × evaluation slots × role productivity, constrained by branch calendars and skill mix.

This calibration creates a defensible bridge between operational workload and labor planning. It also lets product managers test a target change against coverage, cost, and feasibility before rollout.

Product ROI, metrics, and decision support

The platform replaces intuition-only planning with algorithmic decision support that remains reviewable by people. Core measures include labor cost variance reduction, planner time savings from hours or days to minutes, shift fill-rate improvement, coverage-gap aging, protected-status preservation, and audit compliance.

Every recommendation has context, constraints, and an outcome. That allows the product team to distinguish adoption from value: a frequently used workflow is not necessarily good if it increases variance, while a quieter optimization can be valuable if it improves fill rate and reduces replanning.

Delivery roadmap, change management, and phased rollout

Delivery was incremental by design. A region first received a trusted roster and calendar foundation, then controlled replication, protected leave handling, optimization, and executive reporting. Each phase had a rollback path and measurable readiness criteria so operational disruption stayed bounded.

Excel inertia was addressed through high-empathy UI design and zero-friction UX. Familiar workflows were preserved where they were useful, while validation, defaults, status explanations, and audit trails removed the hidden work that spreadsheets forced onto managers. Regional champions, feedback loops, and phased enablement turned the rollout into a change program rather than a one-time software launch.
