Section 1: Technical Architecture & Systems Engineering

This section describes the platform as a controlled planning system with normalized operational data, explicit scheduling state, protected exceptions, optimization services, and a deployment path designed for reliability.

Enterprise data architecture

The foundation is a normalized schema in MySQL 8.4. Core entities separate people, roles, branches, rosters, skills, contracts, calendars, shifts, leave, assignments, optimization runs, recommendations, and outcomes. Constraints prevent duplicate memberships, invalid branch relationships, overlapping effective records, and orphaned scheduling rows.

Historical snapshots preserve the exact roster, target, and rule inputs used for each planning decision. This keeps current truth separate from historical evidence and makes audits reproducible.

Dual-layer scheduling state machine

Shift is the reusable master template. StagingShift is the dated operational instance with assignment, status, leave interaction, and synchronization metadata.

SHIFT TEMPLATE → SIX-MONTH REPLICATION → STAGINGSHIFT CALENDAR

Idempotent keys and bounded updates make reruns safe, reduce race conditions, and prevent duplicate rows or silent data corruption.

Protection logic and conflict resolution

Approved vacations, half-day leaves, national bank holidays, paid time off, and temporary reassignments are protected operational statuses. Explicit human and legal commitments take precedence over temporary overrides and generated defaults. Conflicts are recorded as explainable outcomes rather than overwritten.

Mathematical optimization engine, M3

M3 models mobile workforce routing as a dynamic programming problem over reachable assignments and time windows. Travel allowance matrices encode movement cost, reach-map graphs encode feasible transitions, and country-scoped batch runs keep calendars, labor rules, and geography bounded to the correct operating context.

A swap-refinement heuristic tests local exchanges after the primary solution and accepts them only when feasibility is preserved and the objective improves, balancing coverage, travel, role fit, and operational penalties.

Vacation-cover recommender

The recommender minimizes expected loss rather than simply choosing the nearest available person. Candidate scoring combines skill compatibility, travel cost, branch impact, contracted availability, continuity, and the penalty for leaving another slot uncovered.

Each recommendation passes through a DecisionOutcome grading pipeline that compares proposed cover with actual execution data, including the eventual assignee, realized travel, accepted coverage, and operational deviation.

Infrastructure and modern web stack

  • Next.js 16 App Router with static export, React 19, TypeScript 5.9, and Tailwind CSS v4.
  • TanStack Query 5 manages server state, cache invalidation, loading states, and mutation feedback.
  • Django 6, Gunicorn, WhiteNoise, and AWS ECS Fargate keep deployment repeatable.
  • Google OAuth 2.0 establishes identity and Django issues JWT credentials.

Section 2: Technical Product Management & Operational Strategy

This section connects the architecture to the operating model: who experiences the problem, how targets are calibrated, which decisions improve, and how adoption is managed across a major European automotive marketplace.

Problem & Architecture Transformation

The product replaced approximately 10 decentralized, fragile Google Sheets with a single unified web platform and a governed system of record. It transformed scheduling from constant manual churn into a one-time baseline configuration per branch. Daily operations now rely on lightweight incremental adjustments for absences, vacations, and unexpected shifts.

The platform also integrates a mobile allocation model that calculates the most profitable placement and routing for mobile workforce units. The model optimizes placement against travel expenses, driving distance, and shift schedules, connecting workforce planning to measurable operational economics.

Enterprise Scale & Role-Based Workflows

More than 1,500 employees across European markets use the application for self-service shift visibility and vacation requests. Role-based access control gives each stakeholder the right level of detail and action.

  • Final Employees, 1,500+: view schedules and submit vacation and absence requests.
  • Operational Stakeholders, including Logistics and Regional Managers: access real-time cross-branch visibility, capacity tracking, and regional operational coordination.
  • Dedicated Capacity Planners: run core planning, roster calibrations, and schedule publishing as power users.
  • Country Administrators: manage granular country-specific configuration, localized targets, regional labor constraints, and custom operational models.

The workflow is layered: employees need low-friction self-service, operational leaders need exception visibility, capacity planners need control, and country administrators need safe local configurability.

Stakeholder Management, Demos & Phased International Rollout

Change management used continuous feedback loops with capacity planners and country leads through early pre-releases. These stakeholders directly co-designed features so the product matched on-the-ground operational realities rather than imposing a generic workflow.

Regular end-to-end demos were conducted in English across multiple European countries. These sessions aligned technical capabilities with regional operational needs and surfaced differences in labor constraints, targets, and operating models before rollout.

Executive communication and upward management focused on clarity, strategic roadmaps, and quantifiable efficiency metrics for senior leadership, including Senior Vice Presidents. The product role bridged executive vision with frontline reality by translating strategy into measurable operational decisions.

Target calibration, ROI, and decision support

Working-day calendars, branch demand, role mix, contract assumptions, and evaluation slots translate operational capacity into defensible role targets. The platform measures labor cost variance, planner time savings, shift fill rate, coverage-gap aging, protected-status preservation, and audit compliance.

Target capacity
Working days × evaluation slots × role productivity, constrained by branch calendars and skill mix.

Every recommendation has context, constraints, and an outcome. This lets product leadership distinguish adoption from value and quantify the impact of better planning.

Delivery roadmap and change management

Delivery was incremental by design. Each market received a trusted roster and calendar foundation, followed by controlled replication, protected leave handling, optimization, and executive reporting. Regional champions, pre-release feedback, end-to-end demos, and phased enablement made rollout a change program rather than a one-time software launch.
