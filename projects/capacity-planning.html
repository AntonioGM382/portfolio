The problem: local spreadsheets, global friction

Each branch could maintain its own view of staffing, but the organization paid the price in duplicated work, conflicting versions, slow replanning, and limited visibility across countries. A manager trying to cover a vacation or change a roster had to reconcile operational reality with a template that was not a reliable source of truth.

The product goal was not simply to digitize a spreadsheet. It was to create one governed scheduling model that branch managers could operate while leadership could trust the resulting capacity, cost, and coverage decisions.

How scheduling works in practice

Shift is the master template: the recurring intent for a branch, role, pattern, or employee. It is where planners define the schedule they want to repeat. StagingShift is the live calendar: concrete rows that managers can inspect, adjust, and use operationally.

Replication looks ahead six months, but it is deliberately not a blind copy. Protected statuses—vacation, half-day, bank holiday, paid leave, and temporary assignments—survive template changes. This lets the organization improve the plan without erasing facts already committed to the calendar.

Normalized data and auditability

The MySQL 8.4 model contains 38 normalized tables covering branches, people, roles, availability, schedules, leave, permissions, and decision outcomes. Normalization keeps planning rules transactional; historical snapshots preserve what was known at the time for payroll, reporting, and audit conversations.

Role-based workflows

Branch managers

Maintain the live calendar, manage exceptions, request cover, and see whether the branch is meeting its staffing targets.

Regional planners

Coordinate patterns across branches and countries, run controlled planning scenarios, and resolve cross-site capacity constraints.

Leadership and operations

Use comparable metrics, governed assumptions, and outcome data rather than asking teams to reconcile incompatible spreadsheets.

The PM challenge was translating these perspectives into one workflow without forcing every user to understand the underlying optimization model.

Decision-support with bottom-line relevance

M3 mobile workforce optimizer

M3 uses a dynamic-programming core and swap refinement to search for workable mobile assignments. Travel allowances, reach maps, and country-scoped runs keep recommendations grounded in geography, policy, and the actual operating region. The value is fewer manual iterations and better use of available people—not an opaque “AI” score.

Vacation-cover recommender

The recommender ranks candidates with a loss-minimization model: it weighs the operational cost of leaving a role uncovered against the cost of moving or reallocating another person. DecisionOutcome then grades the recommendation against Redash actuals using a ±25€ band, creating a feedback loop between the plan and the financial result.

Target calibration

Formula pricing calibrates configured branch rosters against role targets, including working days, 20- or 30-minute slot lengths, distinct-person headcount, and composition. That distinction matters: two rosters can have the same headcount but very different role coverage and operational value.

Impact

The system moves scheduling from fragile coordination to an auditable operating process: one live calendar, explicit protections, explainable recommendations, and metrics that connect staffing decisions to cost and coverage.
